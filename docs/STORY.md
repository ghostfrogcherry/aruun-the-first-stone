# Aruun: The First Stone · Story

Version 0.1 · 2026-11-18 · owner: Bruh (ghostfrogcherry)

This is the story plan: who the player is, how they reach Aruun, and the mysteries the
campaign is built on. It uses the same tags as the [World Bible](WORLD_BIBLE.md)
(**[CANON]** Bruh decided, **[PROPOSED]** idea awaiting Bruh, **[BUILD]** in the game today,
**[OPEN]** only Bruh answers). How it plays is in [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md).

---

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

**[CANON]** (Bruh, 2026-11-18)

A cybersecurity worker spends their days looking for vulnerabilities in computer systems. Then
they accidentally find a vulnerability in reality. The opening is grounded and contemporary
before it becomes alien: no prophecy, no mysterious mentor, no ancient voice telling them
they're special. Just a person, a computer, an experiment, and a mistake nobody knows how to
undo.

The protagonist isn't an archaeologist, a soldier, a scientist or a prophesied hero. They're a
daydreaming cybersecurity worker who writes a program that interacts with the Great Occlusion,
without knowing the Great Occlusion exists.

## 2. Narrative rules

**[CANON]** (Bruh, 2026-11-18) These hold for every scene, quest and line of dialogue.

1. **The AI isn't the villain.** It is not conscious and not malicious. Its unprompted outputs
   are a symptom of something much stranger: they are meaningful without the system
   understanding their meaning.
2. **The protagonist isn't secretly special.** They're a curious person who made something that
   worked for reasons they don't understand.
3. **The software doesn't create Avarra.** It interacts with, reconstructs or exposes something
   that already exists. *Which* of those is true stays open.
4. **The First Stone isn't a convenient teleporter.** It has a real function connected to the
   Great Occlusion (Keth-Avar).
5. **Earth stays important.** The protagonist's old life, memories, relationships and wish to go
   home never become disposable, however familiar Aruun becomes.
6. **No one explains the world on arrival.** No narrator, no character waiting with the plot.
   The environment teaches.

These sit alongside the World Bible's identity rules: the player is not the chosen one, and
the world has its own history that doesn't revolve around them.

## 3. The prologue: An Unexpected Result

**[CANON]** beats (Bruh, 2026-11-18). Scene detail is **[PROPOSED]** where marked.

### 3.1 Before the breach

The protagonist works in cybersecurity. An ordinary life: work, computers, routine, and a head
that spends a lot of time somewhere else. They daydream about systems, worlds and things they
could build.

One evening they start a project with an AI coding assistant ("vibe-coding"): a
**pattern-mapping and anomaly-visualisation tool**. Ingest data, find unusual patterns and
relationships that aren't obvious, visualise them, let the user dig in. They describe
features, inspect the generated code, test, fix, and keep adding more ambitious ideas. It's
just a fun project.

### 3.2 The output that shouldn't exist

The application starts producing results nobody asked for. Not crashes or errors: actual
outputs. The AI generates extra structures without being prompted.

- Patterns repeat across unrelated datasets.
- Individual symbols keep consistent relationships with each other.
- Certain arrangements produce remarkably similar results.
- The structures follow rules, but the rules aren't in the software.

The protagonist assumes a bug, investigates, and can reproduce it. They ask the AI to explain;
it can't. They ask it to fix it; the output only becomes *more* coherent. Eventually the
application produces something like a complete geometric system that neither the protagonist
nor the audience recognises.

This is the first glimpse of **Avarra**. Nobody knows that yet.

### 3.3 The breach

They run the application again. The patterns arrange themselves into something that looks less
like a visualisation and more like an **instruction**.

Interference: the monitor flickers, audio devices produce strange tones, the lights misbehave.
The geometry seems to extend beyond the screen. For a moment, an impossible landscape: green
vegetation, red stone, an enormous unfamiliar sky. Then the room begins to overlap with it.

Not a portal. The Great Occlusion is **failing locally**, and the protagonist is caught inside
the breach. Their last view of Earth is fragmented by the intrusion of another world. Then
they're gone.

**[CANON]** The story never establishes whether the program *caused* the failure, *found* an
existing weakness, or whether *something on the other side responded*. That is the central
mystery.

### 3.4 The First Stone

They wake on a circular stone structure: ancient, precisely cut, covered in geometric markings.
Above, the sky is breaking apart; fragments of another place hang in the air; something
enormous is closing.

The structure activates. Not an explosion: its **carved channels light in sequence**, responding
to what's happening around it. The breach collapses. Silence.

The First Stone is a working mechanism connected to Keth-Avar. The protagonist doesn't know its
name or purpose, or whether it brought them here, caught them in the crossing, or prevented
something worse.

> Did the protagonist activate the First Stone, or did the First Stone activate the
> protagonist's world?

### 3.5 Learning to exist on Aruun

The player gets control. Movement feels different: the body is lighter and jumps carry farther
(0.38 g). The player experiments, finds resources, and learns survival and interaction from the
environment. The familiar human body against unfamiliar surroundings is a constant reminder
that they're not home. Eventually they find a route toward people.

### 3.6 Teshari Confluence

The game shifts from isolated survival into the larger RPG. The protagonist meets the Vaari and
others. They are visibly different: a human, an Earth-born outsider in a world where Earth is
generally believed to be lifeless. Their arrival raises questions:

- Where did they come from? How did they cross?
- Why did they appear at the First Stone?
- Why does this human seem to recognise fragments of a language from Aruun's oldest history?

The player wants to go home. The people of the Confluence can point them toward someone who
understands the phenomenon, which leads into the wider world, the Avarran Lyceum and the Echo
Ruin.

### 3.7 How the prologue ends

The player looks at an unfamiliar world knowing only two things: **they're not home, and they
have no idea what they just did.**

## 4. Two stories at two speeds

**[CANON]** (Bruh, 2026-11-18)

| Layer | What it is | Speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The story the player understands | A person crossed into another world by accident and needs a way home. | From minute one. |
| The story underneath | An Earth-based computational experiment interacted with a system connected to the Great Occlusion, and something on Aruun responded. | Slowly, over dozens of hours. |

The player can spend dozens of hours on Aruun without knowing what really happened in their
home office. Aruun must stay interesting on its own.

### The three mysteries

**[CANON]** as questions. **[OPEN]** as answers, deliberately.

1. **The software.** Why did an ordinary application start producing valid Avarran structures?
2. **The First Stone.** Why did an ancient mechanism respond to something made on Earth?
3. **The Great Occlusion.** Why do two worlds share a language and an ancient system neither
   understands?

They unfold independently and eventually converge. A direction Bruh likes, not a locked
answer: *the program didn't create the problem, it found it. The Great Occlusion was already
there; the program merely knocked on the door.*

### The emotional conflict

**[CANON]** The protagonist wants to go home, even after becoming invested in Aruun.
**[PROPOSED]** Late in the story they may find a way to reopen the connection, but doing so could
expose Aruun to consequences nobody understands. That is the choice the ending can turn on.

## 5. Threads to pay off later

**[PROPOSED]** (Bruh, 2026-11-18) Possible connections, not established answers.

| Seed in the prologue | What it might eventually reveal |
| --- | --- |
| The AI's unprompted outputs | The application was receiving or reconstructing information it was never given. |
| Repeated geometric structures | Avarra has a consistent underlying grammar. |
| The First Stone | The breach interacted with an existing ancient mechanism. |
| The Echo Heart | Ancient structures retain and respond to information. |
| Keth-Avar | The separation between Earth and Aruun is deliberate. |
| The second boss | The breach may have consequences beyond the player's arrival. |

**The recognition moment** **[PROPOSED]**: much later, the player finds a structure on Aruun that
is *exactly* one they saw on their screen before the breach. Not a resemblance: the same
structure. The software becomes important again.

### The thing from home

**[PROPOSED]** (Bruh's favourite) The protagonist arrives with very little, but keeps **their
phone**. No signal, no network, no way to reach Earth. It is a quiet, constant link to home,
and much later becomes an archaeological artefact or a clue to the breach.

**[PROPOSED]** How the phone can work in play without breaking the tone:

- **A battery that runs down.** Charging it on Aruun is a small mid-game discovery (a Fluxwright
  or Resonant device, or an Artificer's work). Until then, each use is a choice.
- **Its photos.** A handful of Earth photos the player can look at: home, someone they miss,
  their desk. Never explained; just there.
- **Its last screenshot.** The protagonist screenshotted the strange output before the breach. At
  first it's unreadable noise to the player too. As they learn Avarra, they can compare it
  against what they find, and one day it matches a ruin exactly (the recognition moment).
- **A camera** for recording glyphs into the journal (a natural fit for the journal of
  understanding in GAME_DESIGN §8.3).

## 6. Why the background matters in play

**[CANON]** (Bruh, 2026-11-18) The cybersecurity background is not a cosmetic detail. The
protagonist doesn't magically understand Avarra, but they're used to investigating systems they
don't fully understand: they test things, look for patterns, form hypotheses, and get things
wrong.

| Background | Narrative use | **[PROPOSED]** Gameplay hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern recognition | Recognising repeated symbols and mechanisms | Glyph matching and the Seen → Guessed → Known ladder; the journal highlights repeats. |
| Threat analysis | Reading enemies and Occlusion signs | Enemy tells and the Occlusion warning ladder; journal notes on behaviours. |
| Systems thinking | Understanding linked ancient machinery | Multi-part mechanisms where one word affects several connected parts. |
| Digital forensics | Reconstructing events from remnants | Echo Heart "memories": piecing together what a ruin replays. |
| Security mindset | Questioning institutions and "trusted" systems | Dialogue options that probe motives; the Lyceum isn't automatically right. |
| Troubleshooting | Experimenting instead of giving up | Mechanisms reward trying words and combinations; failure gives clues, not dead ends. |

**[PROPOSED]** The protagonist's inner voice is a troubleshooter's: short notes in the journal
like "Hypothesis: this mark means *rise*. Tested at the stair: it moved." That replaces the
mentor who explains everything.

## 8. Open decisions

**[OPEN]** Bruh's own three, with a recommendation for each. Recommendations are not canon.

1. **What was the protagonist actually trying to build?**
   Options: pattern-recognition and anomaly-mapping tool; personal simulation or world-building
   app; cybersecurity research tool; something else.
   *Recommendation: the pattern-recognition and anomaly-mapping tool.* It's already the draft,
   it explains why the output looks like structured symbols, and it ties straight into the
   pattern-recognition gameplay.

2. **What happens to Earth during the incident?**
   Options: only the protagonist is pulled through; a localised Occlusion forms around them; a
   larger regional event occurs; the incident develops over time.
   *Recommendation: a localised Occlusion forms around them, and nobody on Aruun can say what
   happened on the Earth side.* It matches Occlusion canon (two places briefly sharing space,
   Earth pieces intruding), keeps the prologue intimate, and leaves room for "the incident
   developed over time" to become a later reveal (possibly what boss 2 exposes).

3. **What role does the AI play?**
   Options: unknowingly generates valid instructions; produces unrequested outputs; appears to
   communicate with something; keep it ambiguous.
   *Recommendation: it produces unrequested outputs that turn out to be valid instructions, and
   it never understands them.* That's rule 1 above made concrete. "Appears to communicate"
   risks becoming the rogue-AI story Bruh wants to avoid.

Also open:

4. **The phone** as the one object from home: yes? (Recommended: yes, with the screenshot.)
5. **A playable Earth prologue**, or the title screen straight to waking at the First Stone with
   the Earth part told in a short cutscene? (Recommended: playable, short.)
6. **The protagonist's name and look**: player-chosen (recommended, Elder Scrolls-style), or a
   fixed character?
7. **Wording on Aruun.** The World Bible says the few humans already on Aruun are High Mystics,
   Red Pilgrims and scholars, and that Earth being inhabited is not common knowledge. So do
   people at the Confluence already know humans, just not Earth-born ones? (Recommended: yes;
   humans exist, an Earth-born one is unheard of.)
