# 0004: Art pipeline

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-11-16.

## Decision
- One fixed 64-colour palette (`tools/art/palette.py`, exported to `content/art/`). No textures;
  the look is small voxels, palette and light.
- All art is MagicaVoxel `.vox`. Models (props, plants, items, creatures) use 1/16 m voxels and
  are separate meshes. Structures (buildings, trees) use 0.25 m terrain voxels, are painted only
  in block colours and are stamped into the destructible terrain.
- The game reads `.vox` with its own small GDScript reader and greedy mesher (`scripts/art/`),
  not an engine importer. Creatures are several objects in one file plus a rig in
  `content/models/models.json`, animated with procedural clips.
- The starter set is generated by scripts in `tools/art/`. Hand-edited files win: the generator
  never overwrites a file whose hash it does not recognise.
- Small art (`.vox`, icons, previews) is stored in normal git, not LFS.

## Why
- Nobody on the team paints textures; a palette keeps everything coherent, including imported
  free assets, which are snapped to it.
- `.vox` is what MagicaVoxel saves, so artists never touch code or the engine.
- Our own reader has no dependency on the Voxel Tools version and keeps multi-part models and
  glow colours under our control. It also means the art gallery runs without Voxel Tools.
- Files are a few kilobytes each. Keeping them out of LFS means a plain zip download of the
  repo runs, which matters for friends trying the demo.

## Consequences
- Meshes are built at load time (milliseconds per model, cached). If load time grows, bake
  meshes to `.res` in an editor script.
- Structure colours are matched to blocks by exact colour, so two blocks may not share a
  colour (the validator checks).
- Real skeletal animation is out of scope until after the demo.
